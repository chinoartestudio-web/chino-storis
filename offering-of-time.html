The Story Behind the Artwork

She is a daughter of Cronos, born into a world where time claims everything. Yet in her hands, she holds a sacred fish—a symbol of life, fertility, and renewal—and offers it to eternity. Her gesture poses a question we all recognize: what would we give to protect what we love from the passage of time?

The Creative Process

Meet the Artist

Born in Havana, Cuba, in 1973, Héctor “Chino” Pérez is a contemporary Cuban visual artist based in Miami, Florida. He studied at the School of Design in Havana and later at the San Alejandro Academy of Fine Arts.

In the 1990s, a devastating automobile accident resulted in the loss of his right forearm, the arm with which he painted. Rather than abandon his career, Pérez taught himself to paint with his left hand. This experience became a defining chapter in his life and continues to shape the depth, determination, and emotional strength found throughout his work.

Since beginning his professional career in 1991, Pérez has exhibited internationally in more than 65 group exhibitions and 15 solo shows. His works are held in private collections and institutions across the United States, Canada, Europe, Mexico, Latin America, and Asia. His art has been presented through galleries, international exhibitions, and both private and public auctions.

Beyond his studio practice, Pérez has been deeply committed to community engagement. He developed artistic projects and taught art therapy to children and young adults with disabilities in Cuba, and later collaborated with organizations supporting people with disabilities in Montreal, Canada. After years in Montreal, he relocated to Miami in 2015. In 2003–2004, he was invited to London. He is the founder of Chino Art Estudio1.

Recent auction milestones include Toward Abundance, a circular work measuring 28 inches in diameter, which sold for $13,400 at a private auction on Fisher Island, Florida, in 2025. In 2026, a 40 × 60 inch work from the Tablas series sold for $48,250 at a private auction in The Hamptons, Long Island. His artistic practice continues to evolve through collections that explore memory, identity, transformation, and the passage of time.
